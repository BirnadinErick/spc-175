Rt. Rev. Dr. Orazio Bettachini  -  A Paternal Founder

Rt. Rev. Dr. Orazio Bettachini, an Italian belonging to the congregation of the Oratorians was consecrated bishop in 1846 and two years later he was made the Pro-Vicar of the Jaffna Vicariate. In 1849, he became the first Vicar Apostolic of Jaffna and continued in that office until 1857 when he died rather prematurely when only 47.

Arriving in Colombo in 1842 he went to Kandy in 1843. He felt the need of a Catholic school in Kandy and founded one in 1844 which was the precursor for St. Antony’s College. This school-founding experience had given him confidence to found later on St. Patrick’s College as well as number of other schools. He came to Jaffna for the first time on 30 April 1846. Mgr. Bettachini’s first priority in Jaffna was to establish schools with facilities for English education and refurbishing the already existing Tamil schools. For his plans he was able to count on the support of the Catholic laity in Jaffna. These industrious laity especially those belonging to the Cathedral functioned as a powerful spur to achieve his vision especially through the Jaffna Catholic School Society established by Mgr.Bettachini. The outcome of the tireless efforts made by Mgr. Bettachini as well as the energetic Catholic laity of the Cathedral was the Jaffna Catholic English Boys’ School in 1849.

In the early stages there was no permanent abode for this institution. There was no Bishop’s House and Mgr.Bettachini was staying in the presbytery of the Church of Our Lady of Refuge and the English School was functioning in a temporary building in this Church premises. In August 1849 Mgr. Bettachini took up residence in the house where the present Bishop’s House is. The school was also shifted to the present Cathedral compound in 1850. An Irish gentleman, Mr. Patrick Foy became the Headmaster and there were 50 students.

The Jaffna Catholic English Boys’ School was later christened as St. Patrick’s College on 10th Jan. 1881.
