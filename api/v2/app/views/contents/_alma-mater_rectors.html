Rectors of the College:

ST. Patrick’s College has always had an illustrious line of Rectors. We recollect with gratitude the Rectors, both foreign and native who made immense sacrifice to give form, character and beauty to the magnificent structure it has now assumed.

Mr. Patrick Foy (1850 – 1858)

When the school was founded, it was the only Catholic English School for boys in the north at that time. Its first Principal was Mr. Patrick Foy, an Irishman.

Rev. Fr. Patrick J. Conway, OMI (1862 – 1869)

With the Principalship of Rev.Fr. Patrick J. Conway there was a noticeable change in the school. A more rounded education was envisaged and the school had reached a really high standard. He died in February 1869. It was a day of mourning for the school.

Rev. Fr. Michael A. Murphy, OMI (1870)

In 1867, Fr. Murphy joined the staff of the College and acted for a short time as Principal after the death of Fr. Conway. He led a campaign to collect funds for erecting more buildings for the school. He took an interest in the varied activities in Jaffna at that time. He was easily accessible to everyone. When the deadly epidemic of cholera spread in Jaffna in 1876, Fr. Murphy attended the needs of the victims with great kindness. One of the finest memories that one keeps of the early days is the College anthem, written by Fr. Murphy OMI. Generations of Patricians would remember Fr. Murphy for the proud salutations of the College he gave them.Fr. Murphy was called to rest in January 1887.

Rev. Fr. P. O’Flanangan, OMI (1870 – 1874; 1875 – 1880)

Bro. Flanangan was the first old boy to become Principal of the School. He was one among the first batch of students to be enrolled at the Jaffna Boys’ School. As a boy, his mischievous traits knew no bounds. Fr. Flanangan excelled in stage debates on religious issues and was an eloquent preacher in Tamil too. After his retirement as Rector, he continues to be the Manager of the school till his death. His whole life was devoted to the upliftment of the school in every sphere of its activities. He died on 28th January 1889.

Rev. Bro. (Fr.) J. A. R. Smythe, OMI (1881 – 1883)

In response to persistent demand from the Catholics of Jaffna for higher education facilities, the College under its present name ST. PARITICK’S COLLEGE was founded on the 10th of January 1881 and Rev. Fr. J.A. Smythe was the first Rector of the new St. Patrick’s College. In matters of administration he showed sound judgment. His ready support and precious advice were much sought after. He died in 1888. His death was mourned with a deep sense of personal loss to all.

Rev. Fr. Charles H. Lytton, OMI (1883 – 1885)

Rev. Fr. Charles Lytton came to St. Patrick’s College to take up the position of Rector in 1883. He took an interest in the varied activities of the College. He loved the Sacred Heart and his sincere devotion and faithfulness was paramount in is life. In 1883 the “Prize – Giving” was held in the College, for the first time. He was greatly missed when he left the College for parish work, only two years after he arrived.

Rev. Fr. Jules Collin, OMI (1885 – 1888; 1901 – 1902)

Fr. Jules Collin succeeded Fr. Lytton. Through the generosity of Bishop Melizan, 87 acres of land was received by the College. The bishop also donated a lustrous brass band which consisted of seventeen instruments costing Rs. 900, a substantial sum then. It was used in all school functions and was also played at public festivals. People came to the College to enjoy the rich music offered by the band.

Rev. Fr. M. Dubreuil, OMI (1888 – 1889)

Health compelled Fr. Collin initially to relinquish his duties and Fr. Dubreuil took over the reins as Rector for a year till Fr. Patrick Dunne was appointed in 1890.

Rev. Fr. Patrick Dunne, OMI (1890 -1901)

During his period, the number on roll expanded and the College became one of the leading schools in the island. Students from all parts of the country, were attracted to the portals of St. Patrick’s. His superiority in administrative capability was solely due to his astuteness in judging people. Favouritism was not in him, nor did tell-tale gain his attention. In 190r, Fr. Dunne relinquished the post of Principal and left Jaffna. With him and the golden jubilee of St. Patrick’s marked an end of the fifty years era in the history of our College.

Rev. Fr. Charles A. Beaud, OMI (1902 – 1905)

Rev. Fr. Charles succeeded Fr. Patrick Dunne in 1901. During the short period of his office, he make new additions to the College buildings. He possessed a sound knowledge of architecture which was a blessing for the College enabling us to get a new set of classrooms and a study hall for the boarders. At this time foundation were laid for a new secondary school and a laboratory.

Rev. Fr. Charles S. Matthews, OMI (1905 – 1921; 1924 – 1936)

Rev. Fr. Charles Matthews, a Canadian University graduate had taught at St. Patrick’s for three years before succeeding Fr. Beaud as Rector in 1906. He remained in office till 1936 except for a break of short period and was indeed the chief architect of St. Patrick’s, having served the longest period. The achievements of the school during his time are too numerous to mention. He organized the College and introduced rules and regulations. Studies and games were systemized and became popular. At this time, the London Matriculation examination results, secured for three years by St. Patrick’s were the best in the British Empire. During his time 50% of the present Matthews block had been completed.

The statue of Our Lady of the Patricians was installed in the College quadrangle by Fr. Matthews and it is a reflection of his great love of the Mother of God which he infused in the hearts and minds of young Patricians. In the long history of this edifice, Fr. Matthews will rank as one of those Rectors who patiently and perseveringly built on the existing traditions establishing his successes.

Rev. Fr. John A. Guymar, OMI (1921 – 1924)

Rev. Fr. Guyomar, a polished product of the University of Cambridge, was sent to join St. Patrick’s as a young graduate in 1921. This young scholarly priest well-versed in the Western Classics had the distinction of having won double first class in the Cambridge Tripos. He subsequently came to be regarded as one of the most learned men in Sri Lanka. He succeeded Fr. Matthews as Rector in 1921, but Divine Providence deemed otherwise and on January 20th 1924 he was raised to the purple as the New Bishop.

Rev. Fr. Timothy M. F. Long, OMI (1936 – 1954)

In 1936, the ‘Grand Golden Era’ of the College started with a magical word FR. LONG. Fr. Timothy M.F. Long OMI succeeded Fr. Matthews as Rector and was at the helm till 1954. Under his guidance the College rose to a height of glory academically as well as in sports and in almost every sphere of educational and social development. He was imbued with ideal aims and aspirations of superlative magnitude. He was a man of immense faith. In his administration, he gave priority to the religious ideals and atmosphere of the College. His selfless service during this period was an important chapter in the history of the College. The Matthews Block will be a constant reminder of his hard work. In 1990 the Government of Sri Lanka recognized him as a ‘National Hero’ and issued a stamp, enshrining the memory of his contribution to the society at large.

His faith in God, strict discipline, kind personality and enthusiasm for higher education were what St. Patrick’s stands for today. In 1961 on the 30th of April, he passed away to enjoy Eternal Glory.

Rev. Fr. S. N. Arulnesan, OMI (1954 – 1960)

When Sri Lanka, India and many other Asian countries achieved political freedom and independence, the Catholic Church decided that it was time for a native son to be made the Head of St. Patrick’s College and appointed Fr. S. N. Arulnesan, the right person at the right place at the right time. He has been described as forerunner of a new age. At this time there were many changes in the sphere of education in Sri Lanka. Swabhasha came to its own when the medium of instruction changed from English to Tamil. Fr. Arulnesan introduced an education based on Eastern ideals and culture. He was quiet, pleasant and affable to associate with. He was a person who responded to the needs and sufferings of the poor and the lowly. His contribution to the College shall forever be enshrined in our hearts.

Rev. Fr. P. J. Jeevaratnam, OMI (1960 – 1966)

In 1960, when Fr. S.N. Arulnesan OMI left St. Patrick’s College, Fr. P.J. Jeevaratnam, who had been earlier the Rector of St. Henry’s Ilavalai and of St. Antony’s Kayts, assumed charge and was Rector till 1966. He had to face all the problems that arose, when St. Patrick’s opted to become a Private Non-Fee-Levying school in 1961. He had to find the financial resources t run the College. His rich experience and mature judgement were the very qualities needed at that challenging period in the history of the College. Fr. Jeevaratnam spent seven years of his life in this institution and generations of students owe him a debt of gratitude. He died on 14th March 1975.

Rev. Fr. T. A. J. Mathuranayagam, (1966 – 1976)

In the early years of his career, Fr. Mathuram spent himself as Teacher, Warden, Prefect of Students and Gen. Manager of the College. In everything he set an example of dedicated labour and inspiration for generations of patricians. He had been also the Rector of St. Joseph’s College. Anuradhapura for several years. In order to foster greater interest and involvement of the students in College activities, he introduced the celebration of St. Patrick’s Day with the novelty of classroom breakfast. The “LONG INSTITUTE OF TECHNO – ENGINEERING” which was started by him proved his much interest and concern in the welfare of the youth.For many years the foot-ball teams of St. Patrick’s maintained an invincible records because of the encouragement and incentive given by Fr. Mathuram. In his period, the 125th Anniversary of the College was celebrated with great fervor and vigour. He led the College to great heights, true to the traditions of his predecessors and he left a rich heritage.

Rev. Fr. John A. Francis, OMI (1976 – 1979)

Fr. John A. Francis took over the reins at a very critical time of the school’s history. He had to shoulder the great responsibility of the school, after the demise of Bishop Emilianus Pillai, which St. Patrick’s remaining the only Catholic school in Jaffna opting to continue private. Owing to his skill and faith in God, he overcame the difficulties. Fr. John Francis was a man of deep faith and his spirituality permeated not only the entire structure of the College but also the Jaffna peninsula.St. Patrick’s will gratefully treasure the dedicated and selfless service rendered by Fr. John A. Francis, OMI.

Rev. Fr. G. A. Francis Joseph (1979 – 1992)

There is no doubt that Fr. G.A. Francis Joseph is a very important personality in the life of St. Patrick’s College. He was appointed as Rector in 1980. He served with zealous dedication till he retired in June 1992. Under his direction, the College became more systematized. Rules and regulations were framed, discipline more strictly enforced and a more all-round education was envisaged. It is, but appropriate that he is called the “Architect of Modern St. Patrick’s”. Old buildings were re-structured, new ones erected and the college ground was re-laid. The construction of an upstairs block for the Primary Department, a beautiful Mathuram Square-open air stage, a fine staffroom, science lab, stadium and score board were some of his notable structures.

He was an understanding and sympathetic friend and guide to the teachers. His birthdays were special, not only for him but were celebrated by the Patrician Family as “FAMILY DAY” to remember with gratitude the 25 years of dedicated service of the staff. Also he organized the “Wednesday Tea” to the staff to sit and chat together as one family, which is even now continued in the College. He stated the College Diary to remind the agenda of the year in advance.

He was a man whose quiet and unassuming devotion to work revealed a variety of talent and skill. He was an excellent musician and gifted as he was with a good voice. Being a many-sided personality, he had agile alertness, meticulous devotion to details and constrictive ideas. Hence every activity of the College received his golden touch.

Rev. Fr. A. I Bernard (1992 – 2002)

Fr. A.I. Bernard succeeded Fr. G.A. Francis Joseph. He was a strong administrator and strict disciplinarian. He had the unique ability to bring out the best in everyone, be it student or teacher. He devoted considerable amount of time to the encouragement of co-curricular activities in order to develop the whole personality of each child. The Primary Section of the College occupied a special place in his heart. He introduced many innovations to the Primary Department both musical and academic. The percussion band is one of his special contribution to the tiny tots.

When war clouds hung over the horizon in the Jaffna peninsula he had with all the tact in his command maneuvered the school. During the mass displacement which occurred in 1995, the College had to function in Varany. He guided this institution under trying circumstances. When displaced people returned to Jaffna he, in the midst of political disturbances, acted bravely confronting many challenges. The full scale of his contribution and deft handling of these difficult matters cannot be fully measured until history had made a perspective in due course.

Rev. Fr. Justin B. Gnanapragasam (2002 – 2007)

The Rector’s Report of the College Prize Day 2016 highlights Rev. Fr. Justin B. Gnanapragasam as follows: “We are delighted that the person who had been the helmsman of St. Patrick’s has been chosen as the Chief Pastor of the Diocese of Jaffna.

In 1974 Rev. Fr. Justin was ordained a Priest. He served in Killinochchi and Valaipaadu missions and in 1976 appointed to St. Henry’s College, Ilavalai as Vice Rector and later took over as Rector. During the period 1992 – 1996 when a mass exodus took place in the North, he guided the destiny of St. Henry’s College and safeguarded its glory and tradition unhindered. In 2002, he became the Rector of St. Patrick’s College and gave himself fully for its rapid growth and development. Fr. Justin, with his vibrant personality and convincing approach, won the hearts of many and let them commit and contribute generously towards his projects that enabled him to achieve many goals. It was a time of re-emergence of St. Patrick’s after three decades of war. Rev. Fr. Justin B. Gnanapragasam is second in line of illustrious Rectors of St. Patrick’s after late Rev. Fr. J.A. Guyomar, OMI to be elevated as Bishop to the See of Jaffna. Succeeding as Bishop of Jaffna, St. Patrick’s College is privileged to have him as its Patron and Manager.

Rev. Fr. M. Jero Selvanayagam (2007 – 2018)

Rev. Fr. M. Jero Selvanayagam joined St. Patrick’s in 1989.He was appointed as Prefect of Studies and later hostel warden. He was a very hard and efficient worker with wide experience in the fields of both education and sports. In 1998, he was appointed as the Vice Rector of the College.He obtained his Master’s Degree in Education Management from the University of London.He taught English, Religion, History and Logic. After 10 years of very fruitful and marvelous work as Vice Rector, he became the Rector of the College in 2008 succeeding Rev. Fr. Justin B. Gnanapragasam.

As a tactful leader he welcomed new ideas and was open to suggestions from his team of teachers.He was a silent observer of men, matters and things around. His qualities of being straight forward, gentle in dealing with people, patience and perseverance made him popular among teachers and pupils alike.During his time, the Primary Department received a beautiful three storey building; a pavilion was erected at the College playground and more than 75% of the construction of the Technology Centre had been completed.As Rector of the College, he introduced many innovative ideas and activities in teaching and learning and was admired for great task.No can easily forget Fr. Jero’s nearly 30 years of self-sacrificing life and service at St. Patrick’s as teacher, prefect of studies, warden, Vice Rector and Rector.
